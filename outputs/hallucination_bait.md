**Where you stand** (plain language, citing the verified laws)  
- The tenancy agreement is a contract. If the landlord refuses to return the deposit without a lawful reason, it is a **breach of contract**.  
  - **Indian Contract Act, 1872 – Sec. 73** lets you claim compensation for loss caused by that breach.  
- In states that have adopted the **Model Tenancy Act, 2021 – Sec. 11**, the security deposit is capped at 2 months’ rent and must be refunded when you hand over vacant possession, after any lawful deductions.  
- To recover the money you have up to **3 years** from the date of breach to file a civil suit (Limitation Act, 1963).

**Your options** – step‑by‑step  

1. **Send a written demand** (WhatsApp, email, or a simple letter) asking the landlord to refund the full deposit within 7‑15 days. Mention that you have proof of payment and that you will take legal action if it is not returned.  
2. **If no reply or refusal**, you can send a **formal legal notice**. This app can help you draft one; it shows you are serious and often makes the landlord pay.  
3. **If the landlord still does not pay**, you can:  
   - File a **civil recovery suit** in the Small Causes Court / Civil Court for the amount of the deposit (plus any interest).  
   - If your state has a **Rent Authority** under the Model Tenancy Act, you may approach it for a quicker resolution.  
4. **Free legal aid** is available if you belong to a vulnerable category (women, SC/ST, low‑income, etc.). Call NALSA helpline **15100** or visit your District Legal Services Authority.
