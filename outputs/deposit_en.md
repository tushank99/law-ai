**Where you stand**  
- The rent agreement is a **contract**. Under **Indian Contract Act, 1872 – Sec. 73**, a party suffering loss because of a breach (here, the landlord’s refusal to return the deposit) can claim compensation.  
- A civil claim for recovery of money must be filed within **3 years** from the date the cause of action arose (i.e., from 31 Aug 2026) as per the **Limitation Act, 1963**. You are well within that period.  
- The **Model Tenancy Act, 2021 – Sec. 11** caps residential security deposit at 2 months’ rent and requires its return after vacant possession, **but it applies only in states that have adopted the Act**. Maharashtra has not adopted it, so you cannot rely on that provision; you must depend on the general contract law above.  

**Your options** (step‑by‑step)

1. **Send a written demand**  
   - Draft a polite but firm message (WhatsApp or email) asking the landlord to return the full ₹60,000 **within 7‑10 days**. Mention that you have proof of payment and that you will take legal steps if not refunded.  
   - Keep a screenshot of the sent message and any delivery receipt.

2. **Legal notice**  
   - If the landlord does not respond, send a **formal legal notice**. This can be done through a lawyer or by using a notice‑drafting service. The notice should:  
     * state the facts,  
     * cite the breach of contract (Sec. 73),  
     * demand the return of ₹60,000 within a reasonable time (usually 15 days),  
     * warn that a court suit will be filed otherwise.  
   - Send it by **registered post (speed‑post) with acknowledgment due** and keep the receipt.

3. **File a civil suit for recovery of money**  
   - If the notice is ignored, you can file a suit in the **Small Causes Court** (Pune) or the **Civil Court** for the recovery of ₹60,000 plus interest.  
   - Attach copies of: rent agreement, bank transfer proof, demand letter, legal notice, WhatsApp screenshots, and any other evidence.  
   - The suit is a **money recovery suit** and is relatively simple; the court can order the landlord to pay the deposit with interest.
